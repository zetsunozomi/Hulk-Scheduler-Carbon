\section{Limitations and Conclusion}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Results apply to the declared demand scenarios and A800 profiles, not
production admissions on other hardware. Historical arrivals may encode
user responses to their original systems; holding them fixed does not model
reactions to changed waits. Scheduler settings, placement interference, and
full-state checkpoint costs are unmeasured. An overhead sweep does not
validate restart correctness. The policy still depends on learned queue
relationships and fallible CI forecasts; hidden priorities or changed demand
can invalidate them. A completion budget and empirical miss rate are not a
production SLA.

Complete-node power is unmeasured. The sensitivity certificate assumes
time-invariant scale coefficients in the declared model, excluding power
changes from unmodeled phases or system behavior. Common factors cancel
within panels, not across workloads. Job attribution does not establish what
other users do with released capacity. We make no facility-wide,
embodied-carbon, or adoption-impact claim. Training and profiling overhead
lies outside per-run accounting and must be reported before claiming
amortized benefit.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
\name{} uses a completion-budget slider to control checkpoint-boundary resource
requests without modifying the scheduler. It learns from complete outcomes,
while an exposure decomposition makes unknown node power explicit.

A fixed-scale win establishes an opportunity; improvement over the learned
precommitted sequence is needed to attribute gains to feedback. Claims about
RL also require comparison with explicit planning.
